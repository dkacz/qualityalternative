Honeybees choose new homes through a distributed process of scouting, signaling, and gradually narrowing attention.

The striking part is not that every bee agrees immediately. They do not. The colony becomes effective because it can compare options without converting comparison into permanent churn.

That is a useful model for human attention. Exploration is healthy when it has a stopping rule. Without one, curiosity becomes a loop that keeps asking for one more sample.

The best replacement moment borrows this lesson: offer a small set, choose one, and let the choice end the search.
