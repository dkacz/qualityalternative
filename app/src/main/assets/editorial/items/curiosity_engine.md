Curiosity becomes compulsive when discovery has no clear stopping rule. The user keeps going not because each item is valuable, but because the system makes stopping feel premature.

Finite curiosity is different. It preserves surprise while still respecting closure. A good replacement experience should say: here is one thing worth your time, and when it ends, you are done unless you explicitly choose otherwise.

That design choice is strategic, not cosmetic. If the replacement becomes another feed, it inherits the same behavioral liabilities as the thing it was supposed to replace.
