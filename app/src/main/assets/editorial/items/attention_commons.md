Attention behaves like a shared civic resource. Once it is fragmented, every next decision becomes noisier, more reactive, and easier to outsource.

Platforms built around rapid checking are optimized for repeated access, not for closure. They encourage low-cost entry and ambiguous stopping points. That makes them ideal for moments when the brain wants relief from uncertainty but has not chosen a real alternative.

A replacement product should answer that moment with something finite. The user should feel that there is a beginning, a middle, and an end. That alone changes the contract. A five-minute read can be completed. A feed cannot.
